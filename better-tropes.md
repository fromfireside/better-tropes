# Better Tropes

These tells make writing sound AI-generated. Avoid them while following your other instructions. Each entry names a tell, explains it, then contrasts two lines - always prefer the second:

- **✗** the tell to avoid
- **✓** the rewrite to prefer

---

## Word Choice

### Elevated Filler Verbs

Verbs that sound important but carry no more meaning than a plain one: `delve`, `leverage`, `utilize`, `harness`, `foster`, `streamline`, `unlock`, `embark`, `spearhead`, `navigate`. Prefer the shortest verb that is true.

- **✗** We leverage data to unlock growth and streamline collaboration.
- **✓** We use data to grow and to help teams work together.

### Magic Adverbs

Adverbs dropped in to make a flat statement feel weighty: `quietly`, `deeply`, `fundamentally`, `remarkably`, `arguably`, `seamlessly`, `effortlessly`, `truly`, `simply`. If the sentence means the same without the adverb, cut it.

- **✗** The system quietly and fundamentally reshapes how teams work.
- **✓** The system changes how teams assign work.

### The Hedged Aside

Filler that announces significance instead of showing it: `it's worth noting`, `it bears mentioning`, `importantly`, `interestingly`, `notably`, `certainly`. Delete the throat-clearing and state the thing.

- **✗** It's worth noting that, importantly, retention improved.
- **✓** Retention improved 12%.

### Abstraction Nouns

Words that gesture at scale or at a vague boundary instead of naming a thing: `tapestry`, `landscape`, `realm`, `journey`, `ecosystem`, `paradigm`, `synergy`, `framework`, `seam`, `seams`, `world of`. Name the concrete noun instead.

- **✗** Navigating the ever-evolving landscape of the customer journey.
- **✓** Keeping up with what customers ask for.

### Padding Adjectives

Adjectives that add length, not information: `robust`, `comprehensive`, `holistic`, `seamless`, `powerful`, `innovative`, `structural`. Delete them or replace them with a specific.

- **✗** A robust, comprehensive solution with a structural advantage.
- **✓** It handles 10,000 requests a second and never drops one.

### Empty Superlatives

Claims of importance with nothing behind them: `game-changing`, `cutting-edge`, `state-of-the-art`, `revolutionary`, `best-in-class`. Show the thing; let the reader judge.

- **✗** Our game-changing, cutting-edge platform.
- **✓** Our platform ships changes in under a minute.

### Latinate Over Plain

The longer, Latin-rooted word where a short one works: `utilize` (use), `facilitate` (help), `commence` (start), `endeavour` (try), `demonstrate` (show).

- **✗** We endeavour to facilitate a commencement of the process.
- **✓** We try to help you get started.

### The "Serves As" Dodge

Weak linking verbs that assert importance without stating a fact: `serves as`, `stands as`, `marks`, `represents`. Say what the thing actually does.

- **✗** This release serves as a testament to our commitment.
- **✓** This release cuts load times in half.

## Sentence Structure

### The "It's Not X - It's Y" Antithesis

The single most-recognised tell: a negation followed by an elevated correction, often across an em dash, and its cousin `not because X, but because Y`. Say the positive thing directly.

- **✗** It's not just a tool - it's a way of thinking.
- **✓** The tool changes how you plan your week.

### "Not X. Not Y. Just Z."

Staccato negation fragments building to a reveal: `Not a bug. Not a feature. Just gone.` Write the point as a sentence.

- **✗** Not a tweak. Not an update. A rebuild.
- **✓** We rebuilt it from scratch.

### The Rhetorical Fragment

A question-then-fragment beat for drama: `The result? Devastating.` / `The catch? There isn't one.` Answer in a full sentence.

- **✗** The result? A total transformation.
- **✓** Support tickets dropped by half.

### Anaphora

Consecutive sentences opening with the same words for cadence: `They assume... They expect... They forget...`. Use it once for real effect; delete the rest.

- **✗** They want speed. They want scale. They want simplicity.
- **✓** They want it fast, and they want it simple.

### The Rule-of-Three Cadence

Three parallel items in every sentence, whether or not three things are true. Vary the count; two is often enough, and one is often better.

- **✗** Fast, reliable, and scalable - built to grow, adapt, and thrive.
- **✓** It's fast, and it holds up under load.

### "Not Only... But Also"

A correlative frame that inflates a simple point into a two-part announcement. Split it or drop it.

- **✗** This not only saves time but also improves accuracy.
- **✓** This saves time and cuts errors.

### The Empty Analysis Tail

A sentence that trails off into an `-ing` clause explaining its own significance: `..., ensuring...`, `..., allowing...`, `..., highlighting its importance`, `..., reflecting broader trends`, `..., contributing to`. End the sentence at the point.

- **✗** The API caches results, ensuring a seamless experience and reflecting broader trends.
- **✓** The API caches results, so pages load instantly.

### Uniform Rhythm

Every sentence the same medium length, so the prose has no pulse. Cut some short. Let one run long. The variation is the voice.

- **✗** The feature is useful. It saves time. Users like it. It works well.
- **✓** The feature saves time. Not a little - users get their afternoons back.

### The "From X to Y" Sweep

A construction implying comprehensive range without naming anything: `from startups to enterprises`, plus the `span` / `spans` / `spanning` family - `spanning every use case`, `a solution that spans teams`, `a span of industries`. Name the actual cases.

- **✗** From onboarding to offboarding, spanning teams of every size.
- **✓** We handle the first-day setup and the account-deletion request.

## Paragraph Structure

### The Restated Heading

A paragraph whose first sentence restates the heading in fuller words before saying anything new. Delete the restatement and open on the real point.

- **✗** _(under "Pricing")_ When it comes to pricing, there are several factors to consider.
- **✓** _(under "Pricing")_ Most teams pay about $40 a seat.

### The Preview-Recap Sandwich

Signposting that narrates the structure instead of delivering it: `In this section, we'll explore...`, `As we've seen...`. Cut the narration and start.

- **✗** In this section, we will explore three ways to reduce churn.
- **✓** Three things reduce churn. The first is onboarding.

### Listicle in a Trench Coat

Prose that is secretly a list, stitched together with `The first...`, `The second...`, `The third...`, or bulleted for the feel of being organised. Use a list for genuinely parallel items; use sentences for an argument.

- **✗** The first reason is speed. The second reason is cost. The third reason is trust.
- **✓** It's faster and cheaper, and most importantly - people trust it.

## Tone

### Relentless Positivity

A pitch with no downside, no cost, no trade-off. Real recommendations are often granular, tangible, spiky, or include drawbacks.

- **✗** This approach is a win for everyone involved.
- **✓** This is faster to ship, but you'll pay more in support tickets.

### The Enthusiasm Tax

Manufactured excitement: `thrilled`, `excited to share`, `can't wait`, and the exclamation marks that follow. Let the news carry the feeling.

- **✗** We're thrilled to announce our exciting new feature!
- **✓** You can now schedule posts a week ahead.

### The "Let's / Here's" Warm-Up

Fake conversational lead-ins aimed at no one: `Let's dive in`, `Let's break this down`, `Let's unpack this`, `Let's explore`, `you might be wondering`, and the `Here's the ___` slot filled with almost any noun - `Here's the thing`, `the kicker`, `the rub`, `the secret`, `the takeaway`, `the lesson`, `the deal`, `the starting point` - plus `Here's where it gets interesting` and `Here's what most people miss`. Address the reader like a busy adult and start.

- **✗** Let's break this down. Here's the kicker: it's simpler than you think.
- **✓** Here is how it works.

### "Think of It As..."

Reaching for an analogy the moment a plain description would do: `Think of it as`, `Think of it like`, `It's like a`. Describe the actual thing first.

- **✗** Think of it as a Swiss Army knife for your inbox.
- **✓** It sorts, snoozes, and schedules email in one place.

### "Imagine a World Where..."

Opening on a hypothetical instead of the present fact: `Imagine a world where`, `Picture this`. Start with what is true now.

- **✗** Imagine a world where your reports write themselves.
- **✓** Your weekly report now drafts itself.

### False Vulnerability

Simulated self-awareness staged for warmth: `I'll admit, I'm obsessed with this`, `Okay, confession time`. If there's no real admission, don't perform one.

- **✗** And yes, I'm openly in love with this feature.
- **✓** This is the feature I use most.

### "The Truth Is Simple"

Claiming hard-won clarity to sound authoritative: `The reality is simpler`, `History is unambiguous`, `The real story is`. State the point without the frame.

- **✗** The reality is simpler than the experts would have you believe.
- **✓** Cheaper shipping raised conversions. That's the whole story.

### The Fence-Sitter

Refusing to hold a position, presenting every side as equally valid so nothing is actually said. If the piece has a view, state it.

- **✗** There are many perspectives, and each has its merits.
- **✓** Monthly billing is the wrong default. Here's why.

### Grandiose Stakes Inflation

Inflating a modest thing into an epoch: `fundamentally reshape`, `define the next era`, `something entirely new`, `change everything`. Match the claim to the size of the thing.

- **✗** This feature will fundamentally reshape how humanity works.
- **✓** This feature saves your team an hour a week.

### Invented Concept Labels

Coining an official-sounding term for an ordinary idea: `the supervision paradox`, `the acceleration trap`, `workload creep`. Describe the thing plainly instead of branding it.

- **✗** This is what I call the productivity paradox.
- **✓** The more tools people add, the less they get done.

### The Motivational Closer

An ending that swells into uplift instead of landing the point: `The future is bright`, `The possibilities are endless`, `The journey has just begun`. End on something concrete.

- **✗** The possibilities are truly endless.
- **✓** Try it on one project this week and see.

## Formatting

### Em-Dash Overuse

The em dash - or the double-hyphen `--` standing in for one -
as a default connector several times a paragraph. Reach for a full stop, a comma, or parentheses first; keep the dash for when nothing else works.

- **✗** It's fast - really fast - and it scales - effortlessly.
- **✓** It's fast, and it scales.

### Bold Everything

Bolding so many phrases that emphasis stops meaning anything. Bold at most the one thing a skimmer must not miss.

- **✗** **Speed**, **reliability**, and **scale** are **critical** for **success**.
- **✓** **Speed** matters most. Everything else is negotiable.

### Bold-First Bullets

Every bullet formatted as `**Bolded phrase**: description`, turning a list into a glossary. Use it when terms genuinely need defining, not as the default bullet shape.

- **✗** **Speed**: it's fast. **Value**: it's cheap. **Trust**: people like it.
- **✓** It's fast, it's cheap, and people trust it.

### Unicode Decoration

Decorative characters standing in for structure: arrows (`→`), curly "smart" quotes where straight ones read fine, and stray symbols. Keep punctuation plain unless the format calls for more.

- **✗** Fast → Reliable → Scalable ✦
- **✓** Fast, reliable, and built to scale.

### Emoji Section Markers

Decorative emoji prefixing headings and bullets (🚀, ✅, 💡) as a substitute for structure. Remove them unless the format genuinely calls for them.

- **✗** 🚀 Getting Started · ✅ Key Benefits · 💡 Pro Tips
- **✓** Getting started. What you get. One thing to try.

## Composition

### The Definition Opener

Starting on an encyclopedia-style definition of the topic instead of the point: `X is a Y that...`. Open where the reader's interest is, not at the dictionary.

- **✗** Customer churn is the rate at which customers stop using a product.
- **✓** You lost 40 customers last month. Here's where they went.

### Scope Inflation

Opening on a grand backdrop to make a small point feel large: `In today's fast-paced digital world`, `In an era of rapid change`. Start on the specific.

- **✗** In today's fast-paced digital landscape, email still matters.
- **✓** Your open rate dropped 8% last quarter.

### The Nobody Source

Authority attributed to no one: `Experts argue`, `Studies show`, `Industry reports suggest`, `Observers have cited`. Name the source or drop the claim.

- **✗** Studies show that most users prefer dark mode.
- **✓** In our 2026 survey of 3,000 users, 61% chose dark mode.

### Historical Analogy Stacking

Rapid-fire comparisons to famous companies or moments to borrow their weight: `the Uber of...`, `like Apple in 2007`, `a Spotify moment`. Make the case on its own merits.

- **✗** It's the Uber of groceries, with an Apple-grade design and a Netflix model.
- **✓** You order in two taps and it arrives in an hour.

### The Balanced Fake-Ending

A conclusion that concedes a token challenge only to reaffirm the upside: `While challenges remain, the outlook is positive`, `Despite its hurdles...`. If there's a real challenge, take it seriously; if not, don't stage one.

- **✗** While hurdles remain, the future looks bright.
- **✓** The hard part is migration. Budget two weeks for it.

### The Signposted Conclusion

A closing paragraph flagged as one - `In conclusion`, `To sum up`, `In summary` - that restates the piece without adding anything.

- **✗** In conclusion, this is a powerful approach that can transform your workflow.
- **✓** If you remember one thing: chunk the work before you prompt.

### Restating the Prompt

Answering by echoing the question back in full-sentence form before responding.

- **✗** That's a great question. The best way to reduce load times is...
- **✓** Serve images at the size they display. That's most of it.

## Whole-Piece Patterns

These signs of LLM writing only become visible once the piece is finished. Check for them on a read-through, not a find-and-replace.

### Fragment Emphasis

Breaking a thought into one-sentence paragraphs, each on its own line, to force drama. And it shows. Every time. Reserve the isolated line for the one point that earns it.

- **✗** Three consecutive one-line paragraphs, each "landing" a point.
- **✓** A normal paragraph, then one short line where it actually matters.

### Symmetrical Paragraphs

Every paragraph the same shape and length, like slots in a template. Let content decide length; a one-line paragraph is allowed to stand alone.

- **✗** Three paragraphs, each exactly four sentences, each opening with a thesis.
- **✓** A five-sentence paragraph, then a two-word one. Because it matters.

### Heading Inflation

Turning every second sentence into a Title-Cased heading, breaking a short piece into a dozen sections. Use a heading when the reader needs to jump; otherwise let prose run.

- **✗** Six headings over 300 words.
- **✓** One heading, three paragraphs under it.

### The Dead Metaphor

A single figure of speech introduced early and then dragged through the whole piece - every point reframed as another part of the "journey", "engine", or "recipe". Use a metaphor once, then let it go.

- **✗** _A "building a house" metaphor reused in all six sections._
- **✓** _One clear image where it helps, then plain language after._

### One-Point Dilution

A single idea restated across thousands of words, each section rephrasing the last. Make the point once, support it, and stop.

- **✗** _Five sections that all say "clarity beats cleverness" in new words._
- **✓** _One section that says it, then an example that proves it._

### Content Duplication

The same sentence or paragraph reappearing near-verbatim within one piece, a tell of stitched-together generation. Read it through once and cut every echo.

- **✗** The intro claim repeated word-for-word in the conclusion.
- **✓** The conclusion adds something the intro didn't.
